How it works

Three steps, then it gets out of the way.

The plan is built once and adjusts as your scans change. Nothing is bought until the app can say why.

1

Answer, once

Age, concerns, sensitivity, pregnancy, medicines and what you already use. Each answer changes the plan, and the app shows how.

2

Scan your face

Three photos in daylight, plus a left and right view. Redness, oil, pigment, texture, pores and lines are measured on the phone. If the light is wrong, the scan refuses to run.

3

Follow the week

Morning and evening in fixed slots, active nights spaced by what your barrier can take, with recovery nights in between.

Inside the app

What it actually does

Plan

A routine, not a shopping list

Pregnancy removes retinoids and strong acids. Reactive skin gets fewer active nights. A retinoid user does not also get acids. The reasons are written in plain words.

Chat

Send a photo of your blood test

The values are read on the phone. The app explains what each one means for skin, then suggests what is worth checking next, in order, never all at once.

Products

One photo of the pack

The ingredient list is read on the phone and judged against your skin and your plan. No barcode, no typing.

Clinic

Procedures ranked for you

What the scan points to, with evidence grades, and anything your answers rule out moved aside with the reason. Book a course and the app pauses your retinoid around each visit.

Progress

Photos that line up

Every scan keeps a photo aligned on your eyes, so a before-and-after slider shows the skin changing, not the camera moving.

Shelf

It can tell you to stop buying

The shelf audit finds two products doing the same job, the job nothing covers, and what has expired. When everything is covered, it says so.

Evidence

Graded, sourced, and honest about the gaps.

Every article and every knowledge-base entry carries a grade: strong, moderate, weak, myth or not studied. Where the evidence is thin, it says so instead of filling the space with confidence.

Procedures are written the way a good clinic explains them: what it is used for, how it works, the expected result, downtime, risks, who should not have it, how many sessions, at what interval, and what maintenance costs.

Privacy

It stays on your phone.

No account, no cloud. Photos, scores, answers, blood values and the chat live on the device, and deleting the app deletes them.

Three things leave the phone, and only when you ask: your rounded location for today's ultraviolet index, a product name when a lookup is needed, and the doctor's report when you choose to share it.

Questions

Before you ask

Is the scan real, or a guess?

Real, and limited. The camera measures colour, shine, texture, pores and lines on the phone, in regions set by your own face. It cannot measure hydration or elasticity, so those come from your answers and are labelled that way. A change under five points between scans is noise.

Does it diagnose anything?

No. It sorts findings into home care, professional cosmetology, dermatologist or medical attention, and says when something belongs with a doctor. It never names a disease.

Is the chat an AI that knows everything?

No. It answers from the app's own graded knowledge base and your data, on the phone. That is why it can say "I do not know" instead of inventing an answer.

What does it cost?

During testing, everything is free. Later there will be one clearly named subscription, with the scan, the routine, the calendar and the whole knowledge base staying free.

Which languages?

The interface is in English, German, French, Italian, Spanish and Russian. Articles and the knowledge base are in English for now.

SkinFormula is a wellbeing tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose. Anything that changes shape, colour or size, bleeds, or does not heal in four weeks belongs with a doctor.
